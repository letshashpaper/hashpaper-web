Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023 & Cryptographic Priority

How Indian evidentiary law treats digital records, cryptographic SHA-256 hash values, and independent timestamping in intellectual property, commercial, and authorship disputes.

STATUTORY PROVISION • SECTION 63, BSA 2023

Admissibility of Electronic Records & Hash Value Recognition

Effective July 1, 2024, the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023 replaced the Indian Evidence Act, 1872. Under Section 63 of the BSA (the successor to Section 65B), electronic outputs—such as digital manuscripts, source code repositories, scripts, and cryptographic certificates—are admissible in judicial proceedings when accompanied by a statutory certificate verifying device integrity and output accuracy.

Crucially, the Schedule to Section 63 explicitly incorporates the Hash Value (such as SHA-256) as a primary technical identifier to prove that an electronic file presented before a court is bit-for-bit identical to the original electronic record created at an earlier date.

Pillar 01 • Non-Repudiation

Tamper-Evident Integrity

If an opposing party alters even a single character or timestamp inside a disputed script or contract, its SHA-256 digest changes completely—immediately exposing post-facto fabrication.

Pillar 02 • Prior Existence

Anti-Backdating Proof

Anchoring the SHA-256 hash to both an IST database record and an independent OpenTimestamps Bitcoin ledger proves the exact work existed on or before the recorded timestamp.

Pillar 03 • Trade Secret Safety

Confidentiality Before Filing

Creators pitching scripts to studios or sharing code with investors can establish priority before disclosure without exposing unpublished trade secrets on public registries.
